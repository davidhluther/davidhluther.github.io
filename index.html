What you're looking at

My work is making companies visible to AI assistants. When someone asks ChatGPT or Gemini which payment platform to use, which vendors to trust, or what something should cost, the assistant composes an answer from whatever it can find and verify. Getting a brand named, cited, and described accurately in those answers is the job. It's where SEO was twenty years ago, a new gatekeeper with no settled playbook and a lot of confident guessing. The difference is that I refuse to guess.

Everything on this site runs on the same loop. Build the thing, measure it honestly, get something wrong, write the correction down, and make the correction permanent so it can't happen again. The case studies document the hurdles alongside the solutions, on purpose, because in a field this young, the person who can show you how they corrected course is more useful than the person who claims they never had to.

Nearly all of it costs almost nothing to run. Free tiers, static pages, open APIs, and Claude Code as the entire staff. That constraint is deliberate, partly because it keeps every experiment cheap enough to abandon, and partly because a one-person operation that ships like a team is the point. The two halves below feed each other constantly: the lab is where measurement discipline gets invented, and the day job is where it has to survive an enterprise.

The lab

The passion projects, where I learn how these machines actually work by building their moving parts: a hybrid search engine, a RAG pipeline over a private library, and the forecast models, public scoring rubric, and indexing investigation running live at davessweater.com, plus the governance gates that fail bad builds and the entity-disambiguation fix that stopped an AI summary confusing my consultancy with a firm in England. Public code where the rights allow, near-zero running costs, and the same standards as the day job. The measurement discipline gets invented here first, before an enterprise has to trust it. And much of it was built for real organizations nearby, including grant research for a homelessness nonprofit, the provenance discipline behind a nonprofit board's document pipeline, and recovery planning for small businesses after Hurricane Helene. The whole section lives at the lab.
