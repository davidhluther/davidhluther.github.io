Six business cases

Six problems a communications office has, and what I did about each. The longer write-ups are linked. My employer's results are on my resume rather than here.

  • Discoverability

    Showing Up Accurately in AI Answers

    When someone asks an AI model which company, school, or product to choose, the answer comes from whatever the model can find and check. I built the program that measures how models describe a brand, finds what they get wrong, and traces each error to a source. It began as a one-person pilot and is now a funded channel.

  • Reputation

    One Accurate Answer, Everywhere

    In July 2026 a Google AI summary described my consultancy as a planning firm in England. Fixing it meant correcting the description at its sources. A crisis asks for the same thing, information that is right the first time and identical wherever people find it, and I saw what that takes after Hurricane Helene from the fire department side.

  • AI governance

    Rules for AI Use That Survive a Deadline

    I wrote company-wide AI-use guidance at my employer, represent marketing on its AI Center of Excellence, and wrote governance for the student AI council at UNC Kenan-Flagler. The rule that holds under deadline pressure fits on an index card. Nothing a model drafts publishes until someone checks it against the source, and a correction goes everywhere the error went. On this site, code enforces it.

  • Measurement

    Measuring the Goal, Not the Nearest Number

    At a previous employer, a week spent building buyer education lowered lead volume and raised close rates, because prospects who were not a fit stopped entering the pipeline. Marketing built the revenue attribution itself and owned the outcome. My public forecast rubric shows the same instinct, revised four times because each version rewarded the wrong behavior.

  • Operations

    A Small Team With Agents on the Grind

    I lead an internal team and four agencies. AI models handle research, analysis, and first drafts, so people's hours go to reporting, creative judgment, and relationships. The agent system behind my own work is the same split made explicit, with chartered roles, written rules after every incident, and walls around confidential material.

  • Public trust

    Public Data, Public Trust

    Every month I publish a short economic read on Boone and Watauga County from public records. It labels each figure with its source and date, publishes nothing it has no license to show, and refuses internal-only series in code.

Teams, boards, and service

I report to the CMO and present results to the CEO. I have directed designers every working day for seven years, led a rebrand, run media relations through a PR agency, and handled reputation work for a company in a heavily regulated industry.

Outside the job I am a senior firefighter and EMT with the Deep Gap Volunteer Fire Department, Planning Commissioner for the Town of Boone, board treasurer of the Watauga Community Housing Trust, and executive board secretary at Hospitality House. The planning commission puts me on the other side of the table, where everything I say is on the record.
