Six business cases

Six problems a communications office has, and what I did about each. The longer write-ups are linked. My employer's results are on my resume rather than here.

  • Discoverability

    Showing Up Accurately in AI Answers

    When someone asks an AI model which company, school, or product to choose, the answer comes from whatever the model can find and check. I built the program that measures how models describe a brand, finds what they get wrong, and traces each error to a source. It began as a one-person pilot and is now a funded program.

  • Reputation

    One Accurate Answer, Everywhere

    In July 2026 a Google AI summary described my consultancy as a planning firm in England. Fixing it meant correcting the description at its sources. A crisis asks for the same thing, information that is right the first time and identical wherever people find it, and I saw what that takes after Hurricane Helene from the fire department side.

  • AI governance

    Rules for AI Use That Survive a Deadline

    I wrote company-wide AI-use guidance at my employer, represent marketing on its AI Center of Excellence, and wrote governance for the student AI council at UNC Kenan-Flagler. The rule that holds under deadline pressure fits on an index card. Nothing a model drafts publishes until someone checks it against the source, and a correction goes everywhere the error went. On this site, code enforces it.

  • Measurement

    Measuring the Goal, Not the Nearest Number

    At a previous employer, a week spent building buyer education lowered lead volume and raised close rates, because prospects who were not a fit stopped entering the pipeline. Marketing built the revenue attribution itself and owned the outcome. My public forecast rubric shows the same instinct, revised four times because each version rewarded the wrong behavior.

  • Operations

    A Small Team With AI Doing the Groundwork

    I lead a team of nine. Three report to me directly, and two of those lead teams of their own. AI models handle research, analysis, and first drafts, so people's hours go to reporting, creative judgment, and relationships. The system behind my own work makes that split explicit, with a written job for each AI agent, a new rule after every incident, and walls around confidential material.

  • Public trust

    Public Data, Public Trust

    Every month I publish a short economic read on Boone and Watauga County from public records. It labels each figure with its source and date, publishes nothing it has no license to show, and refuses internal-only series in code.

The AEO work

The day job in more detail. AEO stands for answer engine optimization, which means making sure AI models can find an organization, describe it correctly, and cite it. At Corpay I run it as its own marketing channel, and eight case studies show how, from measuring what the models say to the content and website changes that improve it. The method is public in full. The results are on my resume.

The lab

The independent half. Most of it exists because an organization near me needed something and had no budget for it, so I built it at no cost and learned how the technology works along the way. It includes a research library that answers with citations, a monthly economic read on the High Country built from public records, a records pipeline for a nonprofit board, and a public weather-forecasting site where I test scoring and forecasting methods.
